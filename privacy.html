1. What we collect, and why

1.1 When you browse the site

Nothing that identifies you. We set no cookies. We do log anonymous page-view records — which page, the date and time, and which posted link (if any) you followed to get there — but never an IP address, a cookie, or anything else capable of identifying you or linking separate visits together. See section 2 for exactly what this involves. Our hosting provider may also keep its own standard server logs (including IP addresses) for security and operational purposes; we do not access those logs to build any profile of you.

1.2 When you use the enquiry form

We collect the name, email address and message you type in.

The form is submitted to Web3Forms, a third-party form-delivery service, which forwards the message to our email address. Your submission passes through their systems and may be processed outside Australia. We use them only as a delivery pipe — not for analytics or profiling. Our lawful basis is our legitimate interest in receiving and answering enquiries, or the steps necessary to enter into a contract at your request.

Please do not send information about a specific artwork through this form. Artwork information should only be sent after an engagement begins, when it is covered by the confidentiality obligation in clause B13 of the Terms.

1.3 When you pay the Quotation Fee

Payment is handled by Stripe. Stripe collects your card details directly — we never see or hold your card number. We receive from Stripe your name, email address, telephone number, billing country, the amount, the date, a transaction reference, and the answers you gave to the three checkout questions (whether you want us to start work before your cancellation period ends, your acknowledgement of that right, and — where it applies — your business status and VAT number). Stripe handles your data as an independent controller under its own privacy policy and its own legal obligations, including any automated fraud and sanctions controls it applies.

Our lawful basis is performance of a contract with you, and compliance with our legal obligations (tax records, and sanctions screening under clause B11 of the Terms).

1.4 When you engage us for an Art Review

We collect: your full legal name, postal address, email address, telephone number, country of residence, the photographs and information you supply about the artwork, and the signed Services Agreement. The engagement is conducted by email; the telephone number is used only to contact you about your own engagement where we cannot reach you by email, or where we need to reach you urgently.

We use this only to perform the engagement, to meet our legal obligations, and to keep the records Australian tax law requires. Our lawful basis is performance of a contract and compliance with legal obligations.

This information is subject to the confidentiality obligation in clause B13 of the Terms, which is permanent for your identity (including your address), your materials, and the fact that you engaged us at all. The contents of your Report are confidential for five (5) years.

1.6 What we never collect

We do not collect, and will not ask for, identity documents, photographs or scans of identity documents, biometric information, health information, government identifiers, or any other sensitive or special-category information. If you send any, we will delete it and tell you we have.

2. Cookies, analytics and tracking

This website sets no cookies at all — not essential ones, not analytics ones, not any. It stores nothing in your browser's local storage or session storage, and asks nothing of your device. It loads no third-party fonts. It carries no advertising, no remarketing tags and no tracking pixels. There is no cookie-consent banner because nothing is stored on your device — the one thing we do collect is described immediately below, and does not involve your device storing anything.

Anonymous page-view counts. Each time a page on this site loads, your browser sends us three things: which page it was, the date and time, and the tag from the link you followed here, if any — for example ?src=yt for a link we posted on YouTube. That is all. We never collect your IP address, never set a cookie or any other identifier, and never link separate visits together into a session or a profile of any kind — each record stands alone. A couple of basic checks run in your browser first to skip obvious automated traffic; nothing about those checks involves your IP address either. These records are stored using Amazon Web Services (AWS) infrastructure. AWS's own network layer necessarily sees the network address of the device sending each request, in exactly the way any web server does for any request it receives — we do not record it, store it, or use it, and it plays no part in the page-view counts above. A separate, aggregate limit on total requests to the endpoint protects it from being flooded, without singling out any individual visitor's address.

Videos. Video embeds are replaced with a still image and a play button. No request is made to YouTube until you click play. If you do click, YouTube receives your IP address and sets its own cookies, under Google's privacy policy and not ours. We use the youtube-nocookie.com domain, which reduces but does not eliminate that. If you never click, YouTube never hears from you.

Currency conversion. Approximate Australian dollar prices shown on the site are fetched when the site is built, not when you visit it. The figure you see is plain text already in the page. Your browser makes no request to any currency service.

Outbound links. Bookseller links go to third-party sites with their own policies. We place no tracking on those links.

3. Who we share information with

We do not sell, rent, trade or share personal information for marketing. We disclose it only to:

  • Web3Forms — enquiry form delivery;
  • Stripe — payment processing;
  • our bank, for receiving payment;
  • our legal, accounting or insurance advisers, who are themselves under a duty of confidence, and only so far as necessary; and
  • a court, regulator or authority, where required by law — in which case we will tell you first if we are lawfully able to.

International transfers. We are in Australia, so if you are outside Australia your information will be transferred here. Australia has not received an adequacy decision from the European Commission or the UK Government. When you send us your details or your images, you are giving them directly to us in Australia. Under the European Data Protection Board's guidance on the interplay between Article 3 and Chapter V, that is collection by a controller outside the EU rather than a restricted transfer, so no transfer mechanism applies to it. Web3Forms and Stripe operate their own transfer arrangements. Our Art Review order records are retained by us directly, in Australia, and are not transferred out of it or given to any other party.

Standard Contractual Clauses and transfer risk assessment. Where personal data moves between us and a service provider outside your country — principally Stripe and Web3Forms — we rely on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses, and on the UK International Data Transfer Addendum for UK data, incorporated through each provider's data processing terms. We have carried out and documented a transfer impact assessment for each of those flows, considering the laws of the destination country, the nature and volume of the data, and the technical and organisational protections applied to it. A copy of that assessment is available on request to a supervisory authority.

4. How long we keep things

  • Enquiry form messages — up to 24 months, then deleted.
  • Client Materials (artwork photographs and information) — deleted or returned within 30 days of delivering the Report, or of the engagement otherwise ending, except for one archival copy.
  • Archival copy of the Report and Client Materials — retained in confidence for record-keeping, legal compliance and the defence of claims.
  • Financial and engagement records — at least five (5) years, as required by Australian tax law.
  • Sanctions screening records — at least seven (7) years.
  • Art Review order records — seven (7) years from the date of the order, after which we delete them.
  • Anonymous page-view records (see section 2) — kept indefinitely at present. If we introduce an automatic deletion schedule for these in future, we will update this policy to say so.

4A. Data minimisation — what we ask you not to send

An Art Review needs photographs of an artwork. It does not need photographs of people, and it does not need your files to carry anything about you.

  • Please redact or crop identifiable faces from anything you send us, wherever the artwork itself allows it. Where a face is part of the artwork that is unavoidable and we will work with it; where a face appears incidentally — in a room shot, a reflection, a photograph of a photograph — please remove it before sending.
  • Please strip metadata from image files. Camera files routinely carry GPS coordinates, device identifiers and timestamps. We do not want them and do not need them.
  • Please do not send documents you were not asked for — provenance files, insurance papers, correspondence, valuations. If we need something we will ask.

Image retention. Photographs and other Client Materials are deleted or returned within 30 days of delivery of the Report, or of the engagement otherwise ending. We retain one archival copy in confidence, solely to defend a claim and to meet our record-keeping obligations, and it is not used for any other purpose. We never use client images for training, promotion, illustration or any published work.

7. If something goes wrong — our breach response procedure

A confidentiality incident means any actual or suspected loss, theft, or unauthorised access to, use of or disclosure of personal information or confidential information. If one occurs, we will:

  • (a) Contain it immediately on becoming aware, including by securing or disabling the affected system, account or storage.
  • (b) Assess within 72 hours what information is affected, whose it is, how the incident occurred, and what risk of harm it presents.
  • (c) Notify you promptly, and in any event within 72 hours of becoming aware — telling you what happened, which information is affected, what risk it presents, what we are doing about it, and what you can do to protect yourself. We will do this whether or not the incident is likely to cause you serious harm.
  • (d) Notify the relevant regulator where required — including the Commission d'accès à l'information du Québec where a Quebec resident's information is affected and the incident presents a risk of serious injury; the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner where an eligible data breach has occurred; the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada where a breach presents a real risk of significant harm; and the relevant EU or UK supervisory authority within 72 hours where required.
  • (e) Record the incident in our register of confidentiality incidents, which we maintain and will produce to a regulator on request.
  • (f) Review what happened and take reasonable steps to prevent recurrence.
