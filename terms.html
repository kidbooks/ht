B6. What an Art Review is

A Report is a subjective interpretive work product. It records what one person, using an unvalidated method of his own, thought he could see when he looked at your photographs for the agreed number of hours. We make no objective claim that a Report detects anything that is really there. We do not represent that concealed content exists in your artwork, that our method is capable of finding it, that any mark we describe was intended by anyone, that any other person would see the same thing, or that any statement in a Report could be verified, replicated or tested. Nothing in a Report is put forward as a finding of fact.

Every reading in a Report is our opinion, stated as such, on a factual basis we disclose in the Report itself — that is, we describe the marks actually visible in the image, and separately give our reading of them. You are free to disagree with the reading; the disagreement is not a defect in the service.

An Art Review is an effort-based engagement. We are engaged to devote the agreed Examination Hours to the manual visual examination of photographs of your artwork, organised into the agreed number of Review Passes, and to deliver the Report. Unless the Quotation states otherwise, an Art Review includes three (3) Review Passes. That is the number we quote for by default. Where the artwork, or the Examination Hours you and we agree on, make a different number appropriate — or where you ask for more or fewer — the Quotation and the Services Agreement state the number, and that number governs.

A Review Pass is a manual visual examination performed by the Provider personally and without instrumentation. Review Passes are ordinarily separated by several days, because the method depends on the effect of that interval. For very large and detailed works, a single Review Pass can span multiple days.

We keep a contemporaneous record of the time devoted to each engagement, showing the date of each Review Pass and the hours worked. We provide that record with the Report, and at any earlier time on request.

Unless expressly agreed in writing, an Art Review does not include, and nothing in a Report constitutes: physical inspection; scientific or forensic testing; provenance research; condition reporting; conservation advice; advice on title, ownership or the right to sell; valuation; appraisal; authentication or attribution; advice of any kind about whether to buy, sell, hold, insure or deal with an artwork; or attendance at any sale, hearing or proceeding.
